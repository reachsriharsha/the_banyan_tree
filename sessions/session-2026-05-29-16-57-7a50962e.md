## How the two auth mechanisms work

**`X-Platform-Admin-Token` — a shared secret (what platform endpoints use today)**

- It's *one* string. Set on the backend as env `AKSHARA_PLATFORM_ADMIN_TOKEN`, and baked into the frontend build as `VITE_PLATFORM_ADMIN_TOKEN`.
- Every platform API call sends header `X-Platform-Admin-Token: <string>`. `require_super_admin` (in `app/deps.py`) compares it constant-time to the env value. Match → allowed, else 401.
- It is **not a login.** There's no user, no OTP, no session, no expiry — like a house key everyone with the string shares.
- **Why it exists:** chicken-and-egg. At M1-07 you had to create the *first* tenant and *first* school-admin before any login system existed. A shared secret bootstraps that.
- **Why it's wrong as the permanent model:**
  - No identity — audit/logs can't say *which human* did it (`actor_user_id` is null).
  - The secret ships inside the frontend JS bundle — anyone who opens devtools reads it. Fine on localhost, unacceptable on the public internet.
  - One secret means you can't have multiple super-admins, can't revoke one person, and rotating it logs everyone out.
  - No role information → **can't drive a role-based UI.**

**JWT — real identity (the M1-08 login system)**

- Real login: email → OTP → backend mints a signed **access token** (JWT, 15 min) + a rotating **refresh token** (httpOnly cookie, 30 days).
- The JWT is a signed blob carrying **claims**: `sub` (user id), `tenant_id`, `role_keys` (e.g. `["school_admin"]`). The signature means the server trusts it without a DB hit.
- `require_user` (in `app/auth/deps.py`) decodes `Authorization: Bearer <jwt>` → now you know exactly *who* and *what roles* — which is precisely what RBAC and a role-based UI need.
- Backed by real `UserDirectory` rows (control DB) with email + `role_keys`.

## The one real design wrinkle

A **tenant user** (school-admin, teacher, parent) belongs to *one* school → their JWT has a `tenant_id`, and requests resolve to that school's database.

A **super-admin operates across all tenants** (creates tenants, platform debug) — they don't belong to a tenant. That's literally why the plan (§40) created a **control-plane DB** "for super-admin, tenant metadata, and audit-of-audits."

So "give the super-admin a real login" = **model them as a control-plane user with no tenant**, and their JWT says "I'm a platform super-admin" (`tenant_id: null`, `role_keys: ["super_admin"]`). Platform endpoints then require a valid super-admin JWT instead of the shared token.

## What your plan already assumes (good news — your instinct matches it)

That role-driven sidebar is itself a later story (M8), but the **identity + permission foundation** is exactly this M1-09 work.

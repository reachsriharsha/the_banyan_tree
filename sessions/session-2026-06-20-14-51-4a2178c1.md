Honestly? The idea is sound, and the part you're most excited about — the capex timeline — is the genuinely differentiated piece. But let me push on it, because the framing matters and there are a couple of traps.

## The core reframe

What you're describing is really **three different systems** that people usually conflate:

1. **The corpus** — every filing, raw, catalogued. (This is what we've built. It's cheap and it's the moat — you can't re-derive history you didn't capture.)
2. **Derived events** — structured facts pulled *out* of those PDFs: "Company X announced ₹4,000 cr capex for a Gujarat plant, expected commissioning H2 FY27." This is an extraction problem, not a download problem.
3. **The timeline / state tracker** — stitching those events across filings over time so you see the *evolution*: announced FY27 → slipped to H1 FY28 → "trial production started" → "commercial production commenced."

Keep these three layers separate. People fail when they try to make the downloader smart, or the timeline talk to PDFs directly. The corpus feeds extraction; extraction feeds the timeline.

## On "download everything" — yes, but decouple it

Downloading everything is the *right* call and it's the cheap part — bandwidth and disk. Rough scale: ~1,500 filings/day, mostly small PDFs but with 50 MB+ annual reports in season → **order of hundreds of GB/year, a few TB over the life of it.** Fine for a home server with a real disk; plan for it.

But **"download everything" ≠ "LLM-analyze everything."** Analysis is where the real cost lives — running a model over 1,500 docs/day × 365 is a lot of tokens. So:
- Download universally (already supported via `--download-all`).
- **Extract selectively and lazily**: triage cheaply (category + keyword/regex for "capex / capacity / commissioning / ₹ ... crore / expansion") to flag candidate filings, then run the expensive structured extraction only on those. Capex lives in `MATERIAL_EVENT`, `INVESTOR_PRES`, `CONCALL`, and annual reports — *not* in the XBRL (XBRL is financials/shareholding). So this is mostly NLP over PDFs.

## The capex timeline — the interesting bit

The honest investing take: "capex goes live" is usually *already priced in* by the time it's commissioned — the market saw it coming. The edge isn't the go-live date itself; it's:
- **Detecting the announcement early** (you have the filing the day it drops).
- **Tracking slippage vs acceleration** — management said FY27, now it's quietly FY28. That delta, captured across filings, is a real signal and almost nobody tracks it systematically.
- **Capacity ramp vs demand** — does utilization follow the new capacity.

So I'd frame the product as **"track management's *stated* timeline and how it drifts,"** not "predict when capex goes live." Reconstructing the past timeline is very doable; forward *projection* should just be "here's the latest stated date + its revision history," not a model pretending to know.

A data model that captures this:

```
capex_program   -- one row per project (the hard part: identity across filings)
  program_id, isin, project_name, location, sector,
  announced_date, announced_amount, announced_capacity,
  baseline_completion,        -- what they FIRST said
  current_completion,         -- latest stated
  current_phase,              -- announced|approved|funded|construction|trial|commercial
  status

capex_update    -- one row per mention, append-only (this IS the timeline)
  program_id, filing_uid, update_date, phase, stated_completion,
  amount, capacity, quote, confidence
```

`capex_update` is your Gantt: each filing that touches a project adds a row, and the spread between `baseline_completion` and the trail of `stated_completion`s is the slippage chart. That's the "program-manager view" you're picturing — baseline bar vs current bar vs actuals.

This maps cleanly onto req.md's two-verb design: add a third verb, **`analyze()` / `extract()`**, alongside `index`/`fetch`. The corpus and catalog stay as-is; extraction writes these new tables; the timeline is just a read view.

## The hard parts (so they don't bite you later)

1. **Project identity resolution** — matching "Gujarat petrochem expansion" across 8 filings over 2 years, with reworded names, is the single hardest thing here. Plan for it explicitly (LLM-assisted matching + a human-confirm step for the ones you care about). Don't expect it to be fully automatic.
2. **Extraction noise** — amounts in cr vs mn, phased capex, JV ambiguity. Always store the source quote + filing_uid so every number is auditable back to the PDF. Never show a figure you can't click through to.
3. **If you ever backtest entries** — survivorship bias (delisted/renamed companies) and look-ahead bias (using the *revised* date as if you knew it at announcement) will quietly inflate any strategy. The append-only `capex_update` table with real `update_date`s is what protects you here — query "what did I know as of date T."
4. **Cost control** — tiered extraction (cheap triage → expensive structured pass) and incremental (only new filings) is non-negotiable at this scale.

## The core reframe: capture ≠ estimate

The trap is trying to extract "EPS" directly. You can't, because management never gives you EPS — they give you *fragments* (revenue growth, a margin, a debt-reduction number, a volume target) in whatever unit suits them that day. So split it into two things, same as we did with capex (`capex_update` → `capex_program`):

1. **Captured guidance** — raw, append-only, auditable. Whatever they said, in their unit, with their period and caveats.
2. **Assembled estimate** — a deterministic, recomputable *driver model* that takes the latest guidance + last actuals and **computes** EPS via an explainable bridge.

Guidance is noisy and human; the estimate is math you can audit and re-run. Never let the LLM output "EPS = 47" — it outputs the fragments; *your code* does the arithmetic.

## Layer 1 — the guidance schema (handles heterogeneous units)

The unit problem is solved by making `metric` + `unit` first-class, not by forcing everything to one number at capture time:

```
guidance (append-only, one row per statement)
  isin, filing_uid, update_date, source_quote, page, confidence
  metric        -- canonical enum: REVENUE, REVENUE_GROWTH, EBITDA_MARGIN, EBIT,
                --   PAT, PAT_GROWTH, EPS, VOLUME, REALIZATION, ORDER_BOOK,
                --   GROSS_DEBT, NET_DEBT, INTEREST_COST, TAX_RATE, CAPEX,
                --   CAPACITY, UTILIZATION, MARGIN_GUIDANCE, DIVIDEND_PAYOUT...
  value_low, value_high   -- a range is the default; point = low==high
  unit          -- INR_CR | PCT | PCT_MARGIN | UNITS | RATIO | X (times)
  period        -- normalized: FY27, Q3FY26, "by CY27", "over 3 years"
  basis         -- CONSOLIDATED | STANDALONE
  direction     -- RAISE | CUT | REAFFIRM | NEW | WITHDRAWN
  conditionality-- "subject to monsoon / approvals" (kept, never dropped)
```

So "we'll do ₹5,000 cr revenue at 18% EBITDA margin by FY27" becomes two guidance rows; "PAT to double in 2 years" becomes one `PAT_GROWTH` row. Capture them as-is. The reconciliation happens in Layer 2.

## Layer 2 — the driver model + EPS bridge

Every company gets a small **driver-based model** for each forward period (FY26, FY27…). The drivers are the canonical set that *assemble* into EPS:

```
revenue → EBITDA (via margin) → EBIT (− D&A) → PBT (− interest + other income)
        → PAT (× (1 − tax)) → EPS (÷ shares)
```

Each guidance row **maps to a driver override**:
- revenue / revenue-growth → `revenue`
- EBITDA-margin → `ebitda_margin`
- debt-reduction → `interest_expense` (next period)
- tax-rate guidance → `tax_rate`
- buyback → `shares` ↓; QIP/dilution → `shares` ↑
- capex go-live (from your capex timeline!) → `revenue`/`volume` uplift in the period it commissions

The **baseline** (the starting point for each driver) comes from **last actuals**, which you already have — XBRL from FINANCIAL_RESULTS filings carries revenue, PAT, EPS, interest, tax quarterly. So: baseline = trailing actuals, then apply guidance overrides → forward EPS.

The output isn't a number, it's a **bridge (waterfall)** — and *that's* your "program-manager view" applied to earnings:

> **Your debt-reduction example, worked:** company repays ₹2,000 cr at ~9% →
> **+₹180 cr** interest saved/yr → ×(1 − 25% tax) = **+₹135 cr PAT** → ÷ 40 cr shares = **+₹3.4 EPS**, landing in the *next full year* (partial this year if mid-year).

Every step of that waterfall links to its source guidance row + the filing quote. You can *see why* the estimate moved, not just that it did. The append-only design means when they revise, you get a new bridge and the diff between them — the same slippage-tracking idea, now for earnings.

## Layer 3 — CMP, margin of safety, and the honest framing

Two ways to express the verdict, and I'd build **both** because they check each other:

1. **Forward view:** fair value = forward EPS × a multiple → margin of safety = (fair value − CMP) / CMP. Simple, but the multiple is subjective and that's where people fool themselves.
2. **Reverse / expectations view (the more honest one):** hold CMP fixed and back out *what the market is already pricing* — "at ₹CMP the market implies ~14% revenue CAGR / 22× FY27 EPS; management guides 18%; the gap is your edge (or your risk)." This reframes "should I enter" as "is the priced-in expectation beatable," which is far harder to lie to yourself with than a made-up target price.

Margin of safety then = the distance between *guided/derived* EPS and *priced-in* EPS, expressed as both a discount and the growth gap.

## What this needs that we don't have yet

Flagging the new data dependencies honestly:
- **Last actuals** (revenue/PAT/interest/tax/EPS history) — parse from the XBRL we already download for FINANCIAL_RESULTS. Mostly free.
- **Shares outstanding & net debt** — from financials/XBRL; net debt also from balance-sheet filings.
- **CMP** — a daily price. Cleanest residential-IP-friendly source is the **NSE/BSE daily bhavcopy** (free EOD close for the whole market), not a live quote API. Fits the nightly batch perfectly.

## The discipline (so it stays a tool, not a fantasy)

- **Output ranges + scenarios, never a point target.** Three columns: `mgmt_guidance` / `conservative` (haircut the guidance) / `priced_in`. The spread *is* the information.
- **Conflict handling:** if they give revenue+margin *and* a PAT number that don't reconcile, store both, compute both, and **flag the conflict** — don't silently pick one.
- **Confidence + conditionality surfaced everywhere.** "PAT to double" with a monsoon caveat is not the same as audited EPS; the model must show that it's leaning on soft guidance.
- **Point-in-time:** the estimate as-of date T uses only guidance with `update_date ≤ T`. Protects any backtest of "did entering at MoS > X work."
- **No auto buy/sell.** It computes implied EPS, the bridge, and the priced-in gap. *You* decide. The moment it emits a "BUY," it's lying about its own uncertainty.
